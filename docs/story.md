This is the world behind the board. None of it is required reading — the
[rules](./rules.pdf) and the [player overview](./player-overview.md) stand on

Each fiction term below maps to exactly one mechanic, so the story and the
rules never drift apart.

## The situation

You aren't commanding a fleet in a standard dogfight; you're leading a
high-stakes tactical conquest inside a deadly electromagnetic dead zone.

Heavy particle weapons can't fire into unmapped space, and a Beam built to ride
warp lanes finds none to ride out here — cut off from every net, it's a gun
with nothing to aim through. Your fleet's survival depends on maintaining a
synchronized **Subspace Lattice**: a living signal network anchored to your
flagship and carried outward, relay by relay, by the only hulls built to take
the dark on the chin — your Escorts. Every move you make isn't just
positioning a ship; it's deploying high-powered relay nodes, cutting off an
opponent's power grid, and trapping enemy vessels in your tactical web.

Your goal is absolute mission completion: eliminate the opposing commander in a
ruthless **Surgical Strike**, or saturate the sector in **Sovereign Space**
until their fleet's grid collapses into total blackout. Dig in and commit to
neither, and the standoff can drag on indefinitely — which is exactly what the
**sector clock** exists to end.

## Collapse at Sector 11

Deep within contested territory lies **Sector 11** — a strategic gateway
shattered decades ago when a stellar core imploded. Neither faction can afford
to cede control, and nothing beyond your own Hub's halo is mapped when you
arrive.

When opposing task forces drop into Sector 11, sensors read dark past the edge
of your own formation. A Beam pushed out ahead of that glow is carrying a lance
with no lane to fire down — no lock, no slide, no shot. Your Escorts feel none
of it; they were never built to ride the Lattice, only to lay it, and it's
their job to plod forward relay-hop by relay-hop into ground nobody's mapped
yet. As armadas advance, network fields expand and collide. Where those signals
clash, **Contested Space** sparks — an electronic-warfare dead zone where
tactical dominance is completely neutralized.

### The Sector Anomaly

The exact center of the arena is occupied entirely by the **Anomalous Core**
(the Gravity Well).

- **Lore:** a superdense, collapsed singularity left behind by the star's
  demise. Its gravitational tear is so severe that matter, kinetic ordnance,
  and subspace signals are swallowed whole.
- **Tactical impact:** an impenetrable pillar in the middle of the void. It
  splits the battlefield into two choke points — commanders can't fire or warp
  through the Anomaly, forcing an early choice between pressing one flank or
  anchoring the other to box in the enemy.

## Fleet assets

Every vessel on your grid serves a purpose within the broader signal
architecture.

| Ship | Call sign | Role in the fiction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Command Hub** | Flagship | Brain and primary power anchor. Radiates a wide zone of **Sovereign Space**. If it falls, the surrounding network collapses and the mission fails instantly. |
| **Escort** | Subspace Repeaters | Mobile subspace repeater built for range, not armor — hardy enough to push into unmapped dark with no signal of its own. Alone, its coverage is limited; **daisy-chained back to the Hub**, it pushes active coverage to the front line. Sever the chain and forward units go dark. |
| **Initiative Relay** | Vanguard Node | One extra Escort, granted only to the commander who **commits to the sector first**, as compensation for revealing their hand early. Deployed forward on drop-in for immediate midboard leverage. |
| **Beam** | Lattice Dreadnought | Long-range particle lance whose own thrusters can't carry it into a fight. It **rides the energized channels of your active network**, sliding across light-years in one maneuver — but only inside your Sensor Net. Strand one outside every net and its guns are dead weight. |
| **Infiltrator** | Phase Runners | Strike craft with a localized folding drive; **jumps across the board independent of any signal** to eliminate exposed targets. Entering hostile network space trips a **Target Lock** on its engine coils, stranding it without warp. |

## Securing the sector

Two paths lead to total victory — and a third, grimmer one that can end the
mission even when neither commander is chasing it.

1. **Surgical Strike — decapitation.**
   *Flank the relay lines, clear a firing lane for your long-range Beams, and
   strike the opposing Command Hub. Once the flagship is destroyed, their fleet
   goes dark. Mission accomplished.*

2. **Sector Integration — total dominance.**
   *If an opponent digs in behind heavy shields, force a system lockout. Expand
   your signal chains until Sovereign Space controls 45% of the sector. Hold
   that ground once the sector clock arms, and their systems overload into an
   emergency fleet shutdown.*

3. **Lockout — frozen.**
   *Leave an opponent with no legal move anywhere on the grid and their fleet
   freezes solid — a loss for them, not a stalemate.*

## Fiction ↔ mechanics glossary

Every flavor term above is one game mechanic:

| In the fiction | On the board |
| --- | --- |
| Subspace Lattice / Sovereign Space | Your **Sensor Net** — the projected coverage |
| Anomalous Core | The center **Gravity Well** (blocks movement and paths) |
| Relay chain / "within two light-seconds" | **Linking** — friendly ships within Chebyshev distance 2 of the chain |
| Contested Space | Cells covered by **both** nets; count for neither side's coverage |
| Target Lock | Enemy ship inside your net — reduced to one orthogonal step |
| Surgical Strike | Capture the enemy **Command Hub** |
| Sector Integration | Cover ≥ 45% of the sector after the clock arms |
| Lockout | Opponent has no legal move |
| Vanguard Node / going first | **Initiative Relay** — the first mover's extra forward Escort |

For the exact numbers and edge cases, see [`docs/rules.pdf`](./rules.pdf). For
how a real game unfolds, see the [advanced manual](./advanced-manual.pdf) and
the [Fleet Academy videos](../apps/academy-videos/README.md).
